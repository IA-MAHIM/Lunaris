# How to use LUNARIS — 2026 NASA Space Apps demo

Open your public site at **https://ia-mahim.github.io/Lunaris/**. The website is a six-step workflow; click **Next** or use the navigation on the left.

## 1. Overview

The home screen shows the current selected lunar latitude and exploratory summary. It also clearly displays the default scientific data mode. Click **Launch Mission Planner**.

## 2. Mission Planner

Choose a landing site from 16 options (including real historic sites and explicitly marked hypothetical reference points). Rotate the globe by dragging, scroll to zoom, or click a site marker. Select a date in UTC and mission duration of 3, 5, 7, or 14 Earth days. Or enter your custom latitude and east longitude and click **Use these coordinates**. Then click **Analyze visibility & continue**.

## 3. Visibility Analysis

Inspect the horizon radar for local Sun/Earth azimuth and elevation. The adjacent 3D-looking Sun and Earth are explanatory illustrations. The actual measured quantities are shown numerically. Examine the 3-row timeline (Sunlight, Earth Link, Overlap), scrub or play time, then read the energy model. You can adjust three power sliders and use the optional **Find Windows** date search. Click **Compare up to 4 missions**.

## 4. Compare 4 Missions

Scenario A uses your main planned site and start. For scenarios B, C and D, pick a site and a UTC date, then click **Compare all four scenarios**. Compare sunlight, visibility, overlap, blackouts and the **exploratory** index. The score is not a NASA mission rating.

## 5. Data & Methods

See the active geometry source and disclosures. Visit NASA/JPL Horizons, Quickmap, Moon Trek, LRO/LOLA and CSA reference links. If an actual Horizons cache has been generated separately, import the appropriate JSON file. The default ZIP has an empty catalog and displays openly labeled analytical estimates.

## 6. Final Report

Read the summary of the main mission. Download the **Markdown mission report** (including four-scenario comparison and sources) or a **CSV timeline** (each sampled Sun/Earth angle and visibility flag), or print a single-page summary.
